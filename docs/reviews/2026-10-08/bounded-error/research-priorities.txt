Theoretical improvements to investigate against the native filters
8 October 2026

Reference cores inspected:
PX4 v1.17.0 d6f12ad1c4f70ad3230afd7d86e971421e02fef4
ArduPilot Copter 4.7.1 dbe792162d06cab66c3475fd5556bf7a120f119e
No full nonlinear dominance theorem has been established.

1. Lie error dynamics and the complete biased update

The checked LieErrorEnvelope result derives exact triangular navigation log
error dynamics for matched bias and inputs. Nominal angular-rate dependence
cancels. This supplies an exact propagation envelope in that special case,
rather than an arbitrary quadratic propagation remainder. The actual biased
filter retains nonlinear tangent-bias forcing. The next useful certificate
must combine physical bias/noise bounds, FOH approximation, correction/reset
and delayed publication in one common physical metric. Right-log physical
reconstruction includes pose lever arms; dropping them would overstate a win.

A smaller complete observation/reset remainder can enlarge a certified
convergence region when linear dissipation and disturbance budgets are held
fixed. The existing quadratic/cubic comparison proves that conditional margin
statement. It does not establish the required ESKF or native constants. An
output-only cubic approximation does not cancel bias or propagation defects.

2. Joint nuisance-state covariance for flow/range/height

EKF3 SelectFlowFusion/EstimateTerrainOffset uses a separate one-state terrain
filter (AP_NavEKF3_OptFlowFusion.cpp). This motivates a common-prior study of
navigation, terrain and pressure cross-covariances and correlated observations.
The local linear minimum-variance correction can be compared to a restricted
gain under the SAME actual prior, measurement model and joint noise law. That
comparison would concern local expected quadratic error, not every noisy trace
or the complete nonlinear firmware across different priors/policies.

Current PX4 already includes terrain in its nominal state and full error-state
covariance (generated/state.h, terrain index23). Its optical flow fusion uses
P*H and may retain/update the terrain gain. Simply adding a terrain state is
therefore not a new advantage over PX4. Sequential scalar fusion is equivalent
to batch linear fusion with appropriately independent/whitened observations;
batch syntax alone cannot be claimed superior. Any advantage must come from
more accurate models, retained correlations, nonlinear remainders or policies.

The unmerged joint-terrain candidate still fails qualification in known
development captures. V14 fixes initialization persistence and passes all
24 disabled-option state/full-covariance byte parity controls, but its first
enabled GPS-denied case fails raw full17 covariance positive definiteness.
The campaign stopped on that failure. These are practical correctness
obligations, not evidence for or against an ideal joint-filter dominance theorem.

3. Preserve information through GPS loss/return and publication

For ideal flat-ground flow/range/barometer/magnetic/inertial sensors, absolute
horizontal position has a translation gauge. GPS-denied performance must
retain the uncertainty inherited from the prior GPS history. We should bound
finite-outage growth and recovery without adding fictitious absolute position
information. Preservation of that gauge in every model/Jacobian/reset needs
an implementation audit; no claim that both native filters violate it is made.

Both native cores already use delayed fusion with forward publication. The
architecture alone is not a novel improvement. A prospective advantage is a
tighter bias-corrected Lie preintegration/prediction bound and coherent resets
that preserve useful correlations. Fusion-time bounds must include forward
prediction before being compared to current-time native RMS error.

4. Evidence required for a superiority statement

Specify physical scales, initial uncertainty, finite outage, excitation,
sensor schedule, bounded-noise event and the exact map/branch certificates.
Then prove a valid ordering of actual error measures: a uniform ESKF upper
bound below a certified native witness lower bound gives strict worst-case
separation on the common admissible set. Ordered upper envelopes do not do so.
A common-model MSE comparison is a separate probabilistic route with explicit
moment hypotheses. Strict pointwise improvement for every possible trace is
not the objective of the delivered worst-case theorem; zero-error/equivalent
cases cannot supply a strict gap.

All 42 delivered public theorems are checked, including the adaptive-gain
correction bridge and implementation-defect budget. Numeric implementation
certificates and native separation remain unfinished. The independent
stationary-validation campaign still contains ESKF losses and stays part of
the evidence rather than being filtered out during candidate selection.

5. Lessons from ArduPilot EKF2 to EKF3

This is evolution within ArduPilot; PX4 EKF2 is a separate implementation.
At the pinned ArduPilot revision, AP_NavEKF2_core.h lists gyro_scale[XYZ] and
accel_zbias, while AP_NavEKF3_core.h lists accel_bias[XYZ] and no gyro-scale
states. EKF3 is not simply EKF2 with every state retained and more appended.
The historical motivation for full XYZ accelerometer bias is concrete:
tridge recommended EKF3 on 31 March 2017 for tailsitter hover, where body X
bias matters and EKF2's body-Z-only estimate is insufficient.
https://discuss.ardupilot.org/t/dual-motor-tailsitters/15302?page=3

Current official documentation also distinguishes EKF3's expanded sensor
support, sensor affinity/lane switching and GPS/non-GPS source transitions.
https://ardupilot.org/plane/docs/common-apm-navigation-extended-kalman-filter-overview.html
Delayed fusion and forward output prediction were already EKF2 features.
https://ardupilot.org/dev/docs/ekf2-estimation-system.html

Our interpretation: a meaningful benchmark needs changing attitude, nonzero
XYZ biases, inadequate excitation, sensor faults and source transitions,
besides clean nominal-flight RMS. Three-axis bias estimation alone is not
new relative to our ESKF or current PX4. Additional states require adequate
observability; at rest tilt and horizontal accelerometer bias can be ambiguous.
Broader operational capability does not establish uniformly lower RMS or a
strict theoretical error advantage. The comparison must measure those claims
separately under common assumptions.
