ESKF improvement and native estimator comparison, 7 October 2026
==============================================================

The current ESKF fixes a sensor scheduling problem and greatly improves
high-rate GPS-denied heading, velocity and position. It does not yet beat both
PX4 EKF2 and ArduPilot EKF3 across the requested scenarios. The native filters
retain a substantial high-rate denied position advantage; PX4 generally has
better velocity accuracy. Some ESKF position cases also regress.

Open eskf-improvements.html for all 128 aggregate rows, including horizontal
and vertical position, velocity, attitude and yaw, with the range across seeds.
eskf-improvement-metrics.csv contains the same values. The JSON evidence retains
per-run scores, validation hashes, source pins and native adapter limitations.

Implementation
--------------

The previous dispatcher selected only one aiding source per tick. Frequent
flow updates could starve magnetic and height corrections. The dispatcher now
selects mocap or GPS for absolute aiding, then independently processes fresh
flow, barometer and magnetometer measurements. Held timestamps are consumed
once. Supplemental acceptance cannot clear a rejected GPS anchor or hide its
primary rejection diagnostic. Flow rejection counters reflect flow's own
result even when GPS is the primary source.

acceptedCorrectionCount increments once per fusion instant if any real sensor
correction succeeds; pseudo measurements and zero-velocity updates are excluded.
Per-sensor acceptance flags describe simultaneous corrections. Scalar source,
outcome and NIS describe the first attempted source in priority order, rather
than summarizing all corrections made at that instant.

Heading correction restricts the gain to attitude and gyro bias along the
body-frame vertical. Translation and accelerometer bias remain considered
states: their uncertainty stays in the innovation covariance, while heading
does not directly change their estimates. Generalized Joseph updating uses the
constrained gain. A focused test verifies unchanged considered states, allowed
vertical gyro-bias learning and positive covariance. This addresses divergence
observed after the scheduling repair; it does not imply accelerometer bias is
unobservable under every maneuver.

Optical flow stays in angular-rate coordinates. A small projection matrix
replaces component expressions; its Jacobian maps velocity and attitude to
image rate. Range uncertainty uses predicted image rate. This avoids converting
noisy image flow to velocity before correction. The synthetic benchmark still
reconstructs angular flow from body velocity and range; it does not exercise
raw image noise or correlated gyro compensation.

Repeated covariance transformations use transformCovariance with a materialized
intermediate product and Inline=false. Matrix multiplication order is preserved.
This prevents OpenModelica expression expansion: generated Tests.All function
source fell from about 45 MB to 4 MB, and GCC test compilation from about 418 s
to 9.76 s. The complete final assertion run took 16.32 s on this host with -O0.
Production generated replay code uses -O2. All 72 refactor equivalence replays
were byte-identical to the preceding algorithm version, not the original ESKF.

Algorithm changes use arrays, matrix expressions and explicit helper names.
No explanatory algorithm comments were added. Independent root Modelica
packages remain in use. Upstream PX4/ArduPilot code remains pinned validation
submodules; no upstream source was copied into this repository.

Experiment design
-----------------

All filters receive common deterministic analytic truth and noisy captures.
IMU sampling is 800 Hz; the generated estimator runs at 100 Hz. Profile names
denote flow/magnetometer/barometer rates. GPS is 10 Hz when available; the other
aiding streams remain available during GPS denial. Replays last 60 seconds.
Flight scores use 13 <= t < 60 s, excluding stationary startup. The transition
removes GPS over 25 <= t < 40 s; return scores use 40 <= t < 60 s.

Original captures use trajectory frequencies .25 and .6 rad/s and seeds
7, 19 and 41. Held-out captures use .45 and .85 rad/s and seeds 101, 307 and
911. Frequencies are trajectory parameters, not speeds in m/s. There are 108
held-out runs: three estimators, three scenarios, two frequencies, three seeds
and two profiles. The previous ESKF was also replayed on all 36 held-out cases.
Aggregate metrics are medians of three seed-specific RMSE values, not pooled
RMSE or confidence intervals. This synthetic benchmark is not qualification.

Results and regressions
-----------------------

At original .6 rad/s and 100/50/50 Hz, denied horizontal RMSE fell from
2.5826 to .4934 m, velocity from 1.1026 to .0810 m/s, and yaw from 30.3354
to 1.7392 degrees. Native PX4 and ArduPilot position errors are .1022 and
.1968 m. Held-out high-rate denied position improves from 1.5352 to .7766 m
and from 1.4631 to .7693 m, still behind both native filters.

At 25/25/25 Hz, held-out denied errors are .1222 and .1199 m, better than
ArduPilot's .1679 and .2228 m but behind PX4's .0850 and .0964 m. GPS-aided
held-out median horizontal errors are lower than both native configurations
at both rates and frequencies. This does not establish superiority in velocity,
heading, availability, startup, robustness or overall estimator quality.

Regressions remain visible: original .6 rad/s low-rate denied position worsens
from .3769 to .4654 m while velocity and yaw improve. Held-out .45 rad/s
high-rate transition outage worsens from .0395 to .0739 m. Original .6 rad/s
high-rate GPS position also rises slightly, .0346 to .0369 m. Improved heading
alone is therefore not a sufficient acceptance criterion.

Return-window RMSE describes the entire post-return interval. It does not
guarantee reacquisition delay, reset jumps or peak errors. Per-run scores also
contain recovery diagnostics; native flags indicate use/activation rather than
independently verified measurement acceptance counts.

Preintegration ablation and covariance
-------------------------------------

All 108 final integration ablations completed with finite outputs and zero
reported step-status errors. They compare one held-input preintegral from the
mean of eight IMU samples, eight endpoint ZOH intervals and production FOH.
Filter rate, captures and tuning are unchanged. At .6 rad/s high-rate denied
position RMSE is .49283 m for mean, .49284 m for ZOH and .49336 m for FOH.
Low-rate values are .46494, .46498 and .46544 m. FOH changes yaw slightly but
provides no material position advantage here. These smooth motions do not
establish the result for aggressive coning, vibration or large intersample
rotation; preintegration is not the current benchmark's main performance gap.

Median child CPU time for a whole 60-second CSV replay was .2858 s for mean,
.3358 s for ZOH and .3412 s for FOH in serial runs on this host. These include
CSV handling and the estimator. They are not MCU deadline measurements or a
fair comparison of native estimator CPU costs.

Actual generated preintegrator and prediction covariance functions were tested
with 20,000 independent adjacent-packet draw pairs for each of three motions
and two integration modes. Each packet contains eight intervals; adjacent FOH
packets share an endpoint measurement. Bias random walks are not included.
For nine-dimensional packet error, mean NEES is about 8.94--8.97 for ZOH and
8.05--8.08 for FOH, versus nominal expectation 9. FOH empirical diagonal
covariance ratios are about .90--.95, with whitened eigenvalues about .80--.96.
Adjacent gyro/velocity errors show correlation of about .03 when normalized by
predicted packet covariance. The held white-noise approximation is mildly
conservative here. Shrinking Q blindly ignores shared-endpoint correlation.

Full 15-state descriptive NEES was computed for 36 final ESKF cases using logged
full covariance and known synthetic biases. All covariance matrices passed
Cholesky checks; mean NEES ranged from 3.04 to 10.16. Flight samples are correlated
and initial errors were not independently drawn from the prior distribution.
These are descriptive diagnostics, not independent full-filter Monte Carlo
calibration or proof of consistency under arbitrary motion or delayed fusion.

Native implementation audit
---------------------------

Actual native estimator cores are compared, rather than the partial Modelica
ports. Pins are recorded in eskf-native-audit.json and the manifest:
PX4 f1c0a1f794edf8e5e974b6ed96df3f95eda0df39;
ArduPilot 1511f27194f1dcc3728270883047bdf022b3fd53;
external adapters 3dbaeb98d21c418c895ac5b4402f056c3eba68d3.
Adapters remain in their separately licensed repository.

All 17 ArduPilot input and 17 output schemas, their message IDs and ordering,
and three PX4 aid-source stub field types were checked against pinned upstream
definitions. Frame rotations, magnetic units, gyro units and flow compensation
signs were checked against native interfaces. Material remaining limitations:
- Flow reconstructs image rate from velocity/range rather than actual images.
- Native active flags are not accepted-measurement counters.
- Native cores receive a common commanded stationary startup phase; ESKF lacks
  equivalent explicit at-rest input. Startup bias information is not equal.
- PX4 uses a shortened one-second health interval and explicit origin setup.
- ArduPilot uses horizontal GPS/flow velocity, heading and barometric height;
  vertical GPS velocity fusion is disabled in this configuration.
- Noise tuning and priors remain stack-specific, without optimality claims.
- There is no transport delay, jitter, magnetic disturbance or range dropout
  campaign. Rumoca 0.10.2's multi-clock HorizonEstimator limitation remains.
- Native adapters do not yet honor invalid or missing GPS velocity flags.
  Reviewed captures all have valid three-axis velocity, so present scores are
  unaffected. Fix the adapters or reject those inputs before broader testing.

Validation and next work
------------------------

Passed: final OpenModelica Tests.All with no LOG_ASSERT; actual generated C
multisensor fixture covering loss/return, held timestamps and independent
rejection counters; negative control failing with the previous dispatcher;
72 byte-identical covariance-refactor replays (36 ESKF, 36 UKF); 36 final held-out
ESKF equivalence checks; 108 held-out native/generated scores; 36 baseline
held-out scores; 108 integration ablations; packet Monte Carlo; 36 full covariance
checks; Modelica structure and Python lint/format checks.

Full Rumoca regression passed at the scheduler stage. Final exports and replays
validate subsequent heading, flow and covariance changes. A fresh full local
CI and remote mission qualification have not run for this complete working tree.

Next priorities are stationary-startup information parity and safe bias
learning, followed by raw angular-flow/range/gyro noise and magnetic robustness
tests. Then test delayed arrival, loss/return hysteresis and jump bounds,
independently sampled prior/noise ensembles, more motions and held-out flight
logs. Tune after correcting measurement/model mismatches. Beating both native
estimators needs acceptable position, velocity, heading, consistency,
availability and runtime results across these tests.
