Geometric ESKF experiments, 7 October 2026
========================================

The objective remains outperforming native PX4 EKF2 and ArduPilot EKF3 across
GPS, GPS-denied and GPS loss/return, including velocity, heading, consistency
and recovery. That objective has not been achieved. This review records the
next geometric candidates, including regressions. Both new estimator options
remain disabled by default; changing defaults needs further validation.

The subsequent buffered-horizon versus retrodiction comparison is recorded
in eskf-delay.html and eskf-delay.txt. Its delay campaign compares ESKF modes;
it does not add delayed native EKF2/3 results.

See eskf-geometry.html for the interactive comparison and
eskf-geometry-metrics.csv for all 192 aggregate rows. Each entry is the median
of three seed-specific RMSE values with their minimum and maximum. Native
scores are reused from the same frozen captures, source pins and configurations
audited in eskf-native-audit.json. The previous review contains experiment
design details and all limitations; these experiments do not remove them.

Magnetic-vector output on SO(3)
------------------------------

The new optional magnetic observation avoids Euler heading extraction.
With b = R_hat' B_world / |B_world| and y = B_body / |B_world|, it uses

  residual = y - b
  H_attitude = wedge((b + y) / 2)
  R_measurement = R_sensor / |B_world|^2.

For noiseless y = Exp(-phi) b, residual - H_attitude phi is cubic in phi.
The measured/predicted midpoint cancels the quadratic term. Scaling by the
reference magnitude preserves the measurement information while avoiding tiny
Tesla-squared pivots in float32. Joseph updating and the existing SE_2(3)
right-Jacobian reset remain in use. Sensor covariance must be positive definite.

Tests check cubic scaling, the unobservable rotation about one magnetic vector,
positive covariance, vertical pitch without an Euler singularity, a zero
reference and rejection of a large field disturbance. The vector path does not
execute the Euler-heading observation. Its assumption is a calibrated and
trustworthy local field vector, stronger than knowing declination alone.

Output equivariance and symmetric linearization are motivated by Fornasier et
al., Equivariant Symmetries for Inertial Navigation Systems, Section 2.2:
https://arxiv.org/html/2309.03765v3
That work also separates geometric pose errors from bias-aware symmetries.
The implementation here is a new observation inside the existing ESKF, not a
complete implementation of the paper's bias-aware equivariant filters.

Geometric initialization covariance
----------------------------------

The existing initialization levels the nominal attitude from filtered gravity
and aligns heading from a magnetometer, but retains the configured broad
attitude covariance. The optional alignment candidate conditions the initial
covariance on those same alignment vectors. It retains attitude-accelerometer
bias correlation and the yaw nullspace of gravity.

The gravity residual is f_filtered - R_hat'(-g) - b_accel. Its Jacobian has
wedge(R_hat'(-g)) in the attitude block and identity in the accelerometer-bias
block. Gravity is used once at a successful quiet startup alignment; it is not
a continuous gravity observation during flight. The candidate is skipped for
mocap alignment, disabled quiet gating and timed-out alignment. It does not
add a commanded stationary phase or a zero-velocity measurement.

The filtered-force covariance follows the existing first-order filter's white
noise recursion. For gain alpha and N usable startup ticks, its variance factor
relative to one sample is

  w + (1-w) alpha/(2-alpha),  w = (1-alpha)^(2 max(N-1,0)).

The sample covariance is the configured accelerometer noise density divided
by the estimator period. No sensor noise values were retuned to the captures.
The magnetic seed is conditioned and consumed at initialization, preventing
the held seed from being processed again on the following tick.

The inherited quiet-alignment gate assumes specific force represents gravity.
Magnitude/rate thresholds cannot prove stationarity: constant velocity and
some acceleration patterns can satisfy them. This candidate adds no new
stationarity evidence. Moving-start and model-error campaigns remain necessary.

Results
-------

Original .6 rad/s, high-rate GPS-denied, median of three seeds:
  current ESKF                 .4934 m horizontal, .0810 m/s velocity, 1.7392 deg yaw
  geometric alignment         .3375 m horizontal, .0719 m/s velocity, 1.4248 deg yaw
  alignment + magnetic vector .3407 m horizontal, .0529 m/s velocity,  .9094 deg yaw
  native PX4 EKF2              .1022 m horizontal, .0155 m/s velocity,  .2185 deg yaw
  native ArduPilot EKF3        .1968 m horizontal, .0643 m/s velocity,  .2482 deg yaw

Held-out high-rate denied horizontal RMSE at .45 and .85 rad/s:
  current ESKF                 .7766, .7693 m
  geometric alignment         .4271, .4738 m
  alignment + magnetic vector .3655, .3461 m
  native PX4                  .0922, .0929 m
  native ArduPilot             .1445, .2082 m.

Held-out low-rate denied horizontal RMSE at .45 and .85 rad/s:
  current ESKF                 .1222, .1199 m
  geometric alignment         .1008, .1018 m
  alignment + magnetic vector .3144, .2771 m
  native PX4                  .0850, .0964 m
  native ArduPilot             .1679, .2228 m.

Alignment alone improves aggregate denied horizontal error across all eight
motion/profile combinations. It still regresses some other metrics: held-out
.85 rad/s high-rate outage position changes .0302 -> .0417 m, velocity
.0444 -> .0468 m/s and yaw .2087 -> .3308 deg. At .45 rad/s high-rate denial,
velocity changes .0506 -> .0537 m/s. These are retained in the comparison.

Vector fusion alone and with alignment causes substantial held-out low-rate
position regressions. It must not be selected automatically based on the
favorable high-rate results. Improved angular accuracy does not guarantee
improved integrated position or bias convergence. Full per-seed ranges and
GPS/return results are in the table rather than hidden behind an overall rank.

A bias-coupled retraction was also prototyped with an independently checked
joint covariance reset. Finite differences passed, but its smoke replays gave
negligible gains and mixed position changes. It was removed from the working
tree. Changing retraction alone is not equivalent to implementing a complete
semi-direct or tangent-group equivariant filter.

Validation
----------

- 72 vector-only generated-code replays, original plus held-out captures.
- 216 baseline/alignment/alignment-plus-vector generated-code replays.
- All 72 disabled-option baseline outputs match the preceding validated ESKF
  byte for byte. Final source-cleanup replay hashes are recorded separately.
- 24 full covariance checks across both candidates, both rate profiles,
  all three GPS cases, and selected original/held-out motions. Cholesky checks
  passed; descriptive mean 15-state NEES ranged from 3.08 to 6.69. Samples are
  correlated and priors were not independently sampled, so this is not proof
  of full-filter consistency or a Monte Carlo calibration test.
- New magnetic-vector and alignment-covariance Modelica assertions passed.
- Existing heading-consider and simultaneous-aiding Modelica simulations passed.
- Actual generated C simultaneous-aiding fixture passed all four option
  combinations, including initialization covariance and seed-consumption checks.
- Rumoca 0.10.2 lowers the complete Tests.All model. A sequential tick-variable
  read in the simultaneous-aiding test exposed ED013; expressing the tick
  declaratively fixes it without dropping the assertions. The fixed component
  simulation passes OpenModelica as well.
- Existing independent scoring tests, structure and Python lint checks passed.

Full local CI, complete mission qualification and remote CI have not been
rerun for this complete working tree. .git and the large scratch build storage
are read-only in this resumed session. Small targeted compiler probes used
bounded temporary storage with compiler caching disabled; existing large
captures and builds stayed under $HOME/scratch. No commit or push was made.

Next geometric milestone
------------------------

Implement and independently validate a bias-aware error geometry, not only a
new pose retraction. The propagation, bias coupling, noise mapping, GPS/flow/
height/magnetic outputs, correction and reset must all use the same error
definition. Compare a minimal semi-direct bias formulation with the tangent
group's additional virtual bias state before choosing the production design.
Preserve unobservable directions and validate Jacobians with independent
finite differences and frame-change tests before flight comparisons.

Add larger initial heading/bias errors and more exciting motion families to
expose convergence differences. Then test magnetic disturbances, raw angular
flow/range/gyro correlations, moving startup, actual delayed arrivals and
bounded GPS recovery. Native startup-information and tuning differences still
need a separate comparison. A geometric name or a single favorable RMSE is
not evidence of beating both native estimators.
