ESKF white-noise calibration and magnetic-vector comparison, 7 October 2026
=========================================================================

One original capture's stationary white-noise calibration, combined with the
existing magnetic-vector/alignment options and fusion horizon, substantially
improves configured ESKF performance. Its horizontal-position three-seed median
is lower than both unchanged native stacks in all 36 tested dataset/rate/delay/
scenario aggregates, and in 98 of 108 individual matched seed cases. This is
not a universal estimator win: all four error medians beat both in only 5 of
36 aggregates, some seeds regress, and PX4 return-output smoothness remains
much better. Production noise tuning and geometry defaults are unchanged.

This gain comes from correcting a sensor-noise/configuration mismatch and
using the earlier calibrated-field geometric observation together. It must not
be attributed to the new semi-direct bias retraction, which is disabled here.
The physical process, priors and bias random walks are unchanged. Native stacks
retain their earlier noise tuning; these results compare configured stacks,
not identically tuned underlying Kalman-filter algorithms.

Browse eskf-noise-vector.html for this candidate against native EKF2/EKF3.
eskf-noise.html retains calibrated noise with heading-only fusion, including
its regressions; eskf-noise-alignment.html adds conditioned alignment while
retaining heading-only fusion. Charts cover both fusion modes, all motion sets,
rates, delays, scenarios and scoring windows with three-seed ranges. Sibling
CSVs retain per-seed data. eskf-noise-ablations.csv joins all three prior rest
options, their three noise-calibrated counterparts and native results, for
6048 per-seed/window rows. eskf-noise-vector-aggregate-ranking.json records all
36 aggregate comparisons without selecting a best option separately per case.

Calibration and noise conventions
----------------------------------

calibrate_imu_noise.py reads only the original .6 rad/s seed 7 IMU capture,
using the declared stationary interval 1 <= t < 5 s (3200 samples at 800 Hz).
Adjacent-difference covariance divided by two estimates white sample variance
without requiring a known constant bias or true gravity/attitude. Averaging
three axis variances for each sensor gives the isotropic setting. Continuous
PSD equals sample variance times sample period; its square root is the noise
density passed to the replay. No truth, flow, GPS, magnetometer or held-out
data enters calibration. The single calibration was frozen before the six full
score jobs and is reused unchanged for all seeds, scenarios, rates and delays.

The densities are 5.2685230462579773e-05 rad/sqrt(s) for gyro and
0.001060807282685249 m/(s sqrt(s)) for acceleration. The source capture hash,
full measured sample covariance, time window, period and limitations are in
eskf-noise-calibration.json. The replay's --imu-noise-density GYRO ACCEL
squares these values into the existing diagonal PSD parameters; compare_delay
and evaluate record them. No option retains the deployment's own tuning.
Initial priors and bias random-walk densities are not fitted or changed.

This is a white-noise estimate for this controlled capture, not a robust
flight-noise identification procedure. It assumes independent sample noise
and declared physical rest; it does not infer rest, characterize vibration or
colored noise, estimate bias drift or prove an optimal physical sensor model.
FOH interpolation also gives adjacent packets endpoint correlations; a single
continuous white PSD is an approximation, not an exact finite-rate joint-noise
model. Real deployment tuning still needs real sensor/flight evidence.

The RDD2 ESKF used gyro PSD 1e-4 rad2/s and accel PSD .03 m2/s3, tuned to a real
flight log. They are very different from the synthetic capture's white noise.
The pinned native PX4 and EKF3 sources instead use angular-rate/acceleration
noise standard deviations and multiply variance by prediction dt squared.
The ESKF integrates continuous PSD, whose leading contribution is PSD * dt.
Copying equal numbers between these parameters does not match uncertainty.

A compiled probe executes PX4's actual generated PredictCovariance helper at
5/10/20 ms with zero prior, identity attitude and the default rate noise .015
rad/s and .35 m/s2. It verifies angle and velocity variance scale as rate
variance * dt squared. EKF3's pinned defaults and its explicit dt-squared
formula are inspected separately in source; no isolated EKF3 execution or
native tuning change is claimed. eskf-noise-native-units.json retains native
pins, source/helper/binary hashes and the executed values. At an illustrative
10 ms prediction interval, native effective gyro PSD is 2.25e-6 and accel PSD
.001225, versus RDD2 1e-4 and .03 (44.44x and 24.49x variance ratios).
This illustration does not assume every native prediction interval is 10 ms.

Noise calibration alone regresses original GPS/denied heading and velocity;
this is not a blanket instruction to lower process noise. Conditioned alignment
and a calibrated full magnetic field materially change the result. Full-vector
fusion assumes a trustworthy field model and is not appropriate in an arbitrary
magnetic disturbance; that disturbance campaign is still missing.

For the vector horizon at nominal high rate, original denied horizontal error
falls from .42541 to .06520 m and held-out from .32369 to .07923 m. Against the
old heading baseline (.19239/.43147 m), these are also substantial gains. PX4
is .09688/.09655 m. Original denied velocity remains slightly worse than PX4
(.01624 versus .01549 m/s); held-out denied yaw remains worse than both
(.27295 versus .21153/.22026 degrees). Original GPS vertical position remains
worse than EKF3, and original transition-outage vertical/velocity still have
native advantages. These exceptions are part of the comparison.

No Modelica algorithm source or production default changed in this stage.
The generated estimator export is the previous independently checked export;
only replay configuration and measurement of noise change. Full CI, mission
qualification, fresh native rebuilding and target timing are not run here.
The composed horizon still has the recorded Rumoca 0.10.2 sampled-read ED020;

semi-direct stage; no flight RAM/WCET advantage is established.

Remaining work
---------------

The correction-geometry win depends on a sensible noise model and calibrated
magnetic field. The next comparisons should apply properly converted sensor
noise calibration to all native stacks, retaining their real prediction periods
and documented parameter floors, before attributing differences to algorithms.
Also isolate the ten denied-flight horizontal exceptions and the velocity/yaw
gaps. Include magnetic disturbances, low excitation, optical-flow texture loss,
range faults, vibration and high angular rates; these smooth fixed-field
captures do not establish general robustness. An ESKF current-output observer
is needed to investigate the accuracy/latency/smoothness tradeoff on GPS return.
Covariance consistency and flight-target efficiency need matching scope too.

Reproduction
-------------

Use an owned directory under $HOME/scratch. Build with build.py --eskf-only
--horizon and Rumoca 0.10.2. Repeat separate builds with --geometric-alignment,
then with that option plus --equivariant-magnetometer. Leave --semi-direct-bias
off. Calibrate only original seed 7 imu.csv using calibrate_imu_noise.py
--stationary-window 1 5 --output NEW_JSON. Freeze that output before running
held-out data. Use its printed --imu-noise-density arguments for every variant.

compare_delay.py --stationary-until 13 --repetitions 1 includes all three arrival
profiles. Original arguments: --frequencies .6 --seeds 7 19 41; held-out:
--frequencies .85 --seeds 101 307 911. Covariance checks use evaluate.py on
retrodiction with --scenarios denied --diagnostics --delay-profile nominal,
the same rest/noise arguments, --gyro-bias .0008 -.0005 .0004 and
--accel-bias .02 -.01 .015 for offline scoring only.

report_native_delay.py --eskf-prefix eskf-noise (or alignment/vector suffix)
--review-prefix eskf-noise renders the matching report. Native evidence remains
native-delay-{original,held-out}.json. The new manifest fingerprints current
source, unchanged generated code, configured binaries, calibration, all scores
and review evidence. Earlier manifests remain historical source snapshots.
