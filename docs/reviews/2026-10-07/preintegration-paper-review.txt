Main finding

The retained FOH exponent, coning/sculling/scrolling split and finite-defect
certificate appear mathematically consistent under the paper's affine-input,
real-arithmetic assumptions. I found a substantive implementation error:
preintegrateImuStep differentiated a midpoint approximation instead of the
closed-form physical increments it actually composes. This was corrected in
the Modelica source. The paper should distinguish its exponent sensitivities
from the implementation's complete physical-increment and composition rules.

Findings, in priority order

1. Physical bias Jacobians need an implementation-level validation (pp. 4–5, 7).
Equation (11) differentiates exponent coordinates. The paper correctly says
that physical increments require differentiating the exponential and composing
those sensitivities. The repository previously used midpoint-based velocity
and position Jacobians and an incomplete attitude recursion. Consequently it
did not implement that instruction consistently with its nominal step.

The corrected recursion applies the closed-form increment derivative to the
exponent sensitivities; transports the previous right attitude Jacobian by
the full increment rotation; and propagates velocity sensitivity into position.
Both FOH and ZOH use this rule. The test differentiates three composed steps,
not merely a single exponent, with a nonidentity initial rotation, nonzero
bias anchor, changing three-axis inputs, zero angular rate, and rates on both
sides of the coefficient-series threshold. It checks all five bias blocks by
central differences. The old recursion fails this regression. The tolerance
is 2e-8 in the test's packed coordinates, not a universal unitless accuracy
bound. Published validation should also give separate attitude, velocity and
position errors with their units.

Figure 3 obtains Jacobians by finite differences of each nominal method.
That establishes a nominal-method sensitivity comparison against truth; it
does not independently verify the analytic Jacobian recursion in deployed
code. Add the composed-step analytic-versus-numerical check and pin the source
revision used to generate it.

2. State the order-limit assumption explicitly (pp. 3–4).
The O(T^5) grading holds as T tends to zero with bounded generator coefficients
N0 and N1, equivalently bounded physical input slopes. In endpoint notation,
Delta omega and Delta a are O(T). Holding endpoint differences fixed while
shrinking T makes N1 scale as 1/T and changes the order interpretation. This
is a clarification of the asymptotic claim, not a counterexample for smooth
inputs with bounded slopes.

3. Keep the certificate's scope explicit (pp. 4–6).
Theorem 3 bounds the retained exponential against the prescribed affine-held
flow in real arithmetic. It does not certify interpolation error relative to
an unknown continuous IMU signal, noise, quantization, floating-point rounding,
or a complete composed flight trajectory. The paper acknowledges much of
this in its experiments. Carry that qualification into the abstract/conclusion
and into any description of an online certificate. The current Modelica code
implements the retained step and its sensitivities; it does not evaluate the
finite-defect certificate online.

The certificate proof uses an ODE residual, Gronwall and an exponential tail;
it need not assume convergence of the entire Magnus series. Separate this
fact from the local Magnus-order argument. The r <= 1 condition is specific
to the stated exponential-tail formula; failure is not proof of a large flow
error, but requires a different tail bound or a smaller interval.

4. Integration accuracy does not rank estimator stacks (pp. 5–7).
Table I is an open-loop integration experiment over 10 seconds. It cannot
establish that an entire ESKF/UKF, ArduPilot EKF3 or PX4 EKF2 performs better in
GPS-aided, flow-aided or source-transition flight. The manuscript has no PX4
column. A complete comparison needs identical measured inputs, independent
truth, alignment/datum/latency accounting, actual fusion activity, coverage,
source-loss behavior and reacquisition metrics. The accompanying estimation
comparison addresses a controlled subset of that question; it is not a flight
qualification or a universal ranking.

5. Separate interpolation gains from bracket gains (pp. 5–7).
Classical FOH and the proposed method agree to the displayed precision in
several entries. The paper recognizes that most of the large 6–40-fold gains
come from replacing a held-input approximation with FOH interpolation. Keep
that attribution visible. Report an averaged-input exact held step, an FOH
step without the commutator, and the complete FOH step on identical endpoints.
State exactly which endpoint or mean each ZOH comparator holds. More digits
or an explicit difference column would show the incremental bracket benefit
without attributing all interpolation gains to the new representation.

6. Narrow claims about classical methods (pp. 4, 7).
A particular classical accumulator may omit a position channel, exact bias
Jacobians or a computable bound; classical strapdown methods as a class do
not lack all of these. Savage's position/scrolling work is already cited.
Tie the claim to the tested implementation and the specific combination of
properties, rather than saying no classical accumulator provides them.

7. Add position-channel and unit-aware evidence (pp. 5–7).
The explicit position/scrolling channel is a stated contribution. Include its
nominal error and bias Jacobian error separately, alongside attitude and
velocity. A mixed Frobenius norm combines metres, metres/second and radians;
it can be useful after declaring a scaling, but should not be the only metric.

8. Make reproduction immutable (pp. 5–8).
Pin firmware and model commits, compiler release/build, sample timestamp
semantics, FIFO/rate-reduction method, filters, noise/capture seeds and sensor
parameters. A moving master URL with an access date does not fix an algorithm.
Distinguish the continuous-signal truth reference from the affine-held flow
reference used for the certificate. Specify solver tolerances/reference error
so a certificate ratio near one can be interpreted. Ratios at the documented
floating-point floor should not be treated as proof tests.

9. Correct a definite typesetting error (p. 4).
The definition of K prints ||N||_0 + T ||N1||. It should read
K = ||N0|| + T ||N1||.

10. Clarify dimensionless versus physical angular notation (p. 2).
Proposition 1 exponentiates an already time-scaled algebra element. Distinguish
its rotation magnitude from a physical angular-rate magnitude multiplied by
T so units and the coefficient arguments are unambiguous.

11. Use the newer PDF for circulation (p. 7).
The older download shows a missing Figure 3 asset / filename in its place.
The newer copy renders Figure 3. Main technical text is substantially the
same; this rendering defect should not be reported against the newer copy.

Checks performed

- Read both PDFs and compared extracted text; inspected the relevant rendered
  equation and figure pages.
- Independently constructed the right-flow ODE Taylor coefficients in exact
  rational arithmetic for 12 deterministic matrix cases of dimensions 2, 3
  and 5. Grades zero through four of Y(T)-exp(P(T)) vanish exactly, and grade
  five matches -[N1,[N0,N1]]/240-[N0,[N0,[N0,N1]]]/720. This is an independent
  sanity check, not a general proof or a numerical certificate.
- Added the composed finite-difference Modelica regression and verified that
  the previous implementation fails it. The complete OpenModelica regression
  suite passes with the correction.
- Exported the corrected predictor using unmodified released Rumoca 0.10.2.
  Whole-array coefficient assignments avoid an upstream indexed-write export
  failure; fixed outer-product matrices avoid an unsupported export builtin.
