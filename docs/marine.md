# Marine simulation

`Marine.Sailing.Sailboat` models a Pilothouse-inspired robotic sloop with tensioned sail membranes, compliant pull-only sheets, a keel, rudder and sensor-driven helm. `Marine.Sailing.Hobie16` represents a full-size catamaran with separate immersed hull sections, twin rudders, moving ballast and main and jib travelers. Both models use metres, seconds and radians. Vessel horizontal position is east and north, with positive height upward. [Pilothouse robotic sailboat](https://srlm.io/2015/07/02/introducing-pilothouse-a-robotic-sailboat/)

For a coupled surface run, send heading, heel, pitch, east, north, the two rows of `sectionVolume`, `stationX`, `beamHalf` and `halfSpacing` to the corresponding inputs of `Marine.Environment.WakeSpectrum`. Set mean wind and current in the same ENU frame. After each 0.1 s wave step, feed `waveLoad` to the vessel’s `wakeEast`, `wakeNorth` and `wakeYaw` inputs. Feed `incident` to `waveHeight`, `wavePitch` and `waveRoll`. The `height` output is the combined sea in metres, row north and column east. Its periodic origin translates with `driftE` and `driftN`.

Each immersed strip contributes its volume, position, width and length to the wave spectrum. Phase differences between port and starboard produce interference. The model reconstructs the surface by inverse FFT and integrates horizontal wave-making force and yaw moment over the same modes. The default 64 m tile resolves wavelengths down to approximately 2 m. The separate `HullWake` model retains a nondispersive near-field comparison solver. A hull design study needs independent resistance, trim and wave-cut measurements, grid convergence, and assessment of periodic return waves. [Catamaran tow experiments](https://eprints.soton.ac.uk/462776/)

`Marine.Wreck.Plant` includes MultiBodyLie dynamics, pressure-dependent ballast pumping, thruster immersion, current-relative drag and contact. `Marine.Wreck.Navigation` models sensor acquisition, vision features and fusion. The submarine design draws on the Piccard work. These two exports and the standalone `Marine.Environment.WaterColumn` are under Rumoca compiler qualification. [Piccard](https://piccard.science/)

`Marine.Rdd2Survey` runs a survey-area quadrotor using the RDD2 plant. It adds a cut-motor input, surface immersion and a flooded-frame buoyancy assumption. Its displacement and drag coefficients are estimates. The RDD2 environment inputs default to a still-air flight environment for existing users of `Vehicles.Rdd2.Plant`.
