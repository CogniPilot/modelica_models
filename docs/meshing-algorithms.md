# Choosing mesh algorithms for propellers, drone wakes, sails and flutter

Engineering review, 24 November 2026. The design question is which mesh and geometry operations a reusable Modelica library should supply for finite-volume CFD and moving fluid–structure interfaces. The evidence below combines original algorithm papers, experimental benchmarks and vendor documentation. Vendor manuals establish what users can configure. The experimental benchmarks establish what can be measured. This is a targeted literature review. The proposed acceptance tests are our engineering judgments.

## Decisions for the library

| Problem | Preferred starting method | Support in this contribution | Principal open requirement |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| External flow around closed solids | Locally refined hex-dominant core with surface snapping and wall layers | Native generator, dihedral-based refinement, rectangular refinement volumes and native OpenFOAM export | Feature preservation, automatic gap sizing, and quality-controlled termination of individual layers |
| Complex geometry needing tetrahedra | Constrained or restricted Delaunay for clean geometry, envelope-based tet meshing for imperfect surfaces | Import conforming linear tetrahedra and physical face IDs into the same volume-mesh representation. Gmsh HXT tested end to end | CAD/surface conditioning, automated generator/file adapters and geometry-error reports |
| High-aspect-ratio wing and blade regions | Swept structured sections or hybrid advancing layers | Native single-block loft with caller-defined section coordinates and spacing | Automatic blocking, periodic seams, block coupling and collision handling |
| Interacting wakes | Directional refinement driven by a flow field or an output-error estimate | Static refinement boxes can cover the near wake | Directional metrics, adaptation during a run, and conservative field transfer |
| Wing flutter or a flexible sail | Boundary-fitted moving mesh, work-consistent coupling and time-accurate flow | Face sweeps for prescribed linear node trajectories, verified per cell | Motion solver, conservative interface transfer, and a coupled aeroelastic validation |
| Large cases | Sparse balanced octrees or partitioned parallel unstructured meshing | Serial native meshing and indexed tetrahedral import | Memory scaling, distributed ownership and parallel performance evidence |

## Algorithms and trade-offs

**Geometry and predicates.** The sign of an orientation or in-sphere determinant determines connectivity. Shewchuk's adaptive predicates evaluate easy inputs cheaply and increase precision near degeneracy. For a clean, constrained domain, CGAL protects feature curves during restricted Delaunay refinement, then improves slivers that pass radius-edge criteria. For imperfect triangle soups, fTetWild offers a different design point, with a tolerance envelope around the input. A tolerance comparable to the thickness of a trailing edge can consume a feature the solver needs. For this library, report watertightness, feature survival and bidirectional distance to the input in physical units. The current Cartesian classifier expects closed solids and uses floating-point ray intersections. [Shewchuk, robust predicates](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/~quake/robust.html) · [CGAL Mesh_3](https://doc.cgal.org/6.1/Mesh_3/index.html) · [Hu et al., fTetWild](https://doi.org/10.1145/3386569.3392385)

**Hex-dominant, structured and tetrahedral methods.** A split-hex mesh has a convenient Cartesian far field. Near thin or oblique surfaces it needs snapping, explicit edge handling and enough resolution across gaps. OpenFOAM exposes distinct controls for these operations. A structured sweep gives direct control over spacing and alignment along an extrudable region, at the cost of selecting corresponding sections and block topology. The all-hex literature identifies automatic feature-aligned blocking for arbitrary geometry as an open problem. For a tet core, HXT combines distributed work across threads with local cavity improvement; its end-to-end mesh-quality benchmarks are more relevant to users than Delaunay insertion throughput alone. Our native loft and indexed-tet interface let users exercise these alternatives today. [OpenFOAM meshing stages](https://doc.openfoam.com/2606/tools/pre-processing/mesh/generation/snappyhexmesh/) · [Pietroni et al., hex meshing survey](https://doi.org/10.1145/3554920) · [Marot and Remacle, HXT](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.08508)

**Sizing and boundary layers.** Curvature sizing limits surface approximation error. Proximity sizing aims to retain a requested number of cells across a gap. A single global minimum edge length does little to identify which gaps matter. At viscous walls, prescribe first-layer height from the intended wall treatment and estimated friction velocity, choose total thickness and growth, then inspect achieved wall distance and coverage. Advancing layers need front–front intersection handling near the sail slot, a blade root or a wing junction. Ye et al. (2025) explore bijective mapping to retain full prism layers. A useful comparison would measure wall coverage, achieved first-layer height, minimum Jacobian and downstream force convergence on the same geometry. The native layer implementation presently uses a common extrusion length, with a direction derived from incident face normals. It can produce low-quality cells in difficult regions. [Ansys sizing controls](https://ansyshelp.ansys.com/public/Views/Secured/corp/v251/en/wb_msh/msh_sf_uniform_curv_prox.html) · [Advancing layers with intersection checks](https://www.sciopen.com/article/10.1016/j.cja.2023.05.018) · [Ye et al., full-layer prismatic meshing](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/pii/S0021999125000270)

**Anisotropic and goal-oriented adaptation.** A positive-definite metric tensor specifies desired edge lengths and orientations. Refining across a shear layer while retaining long cells along it can reduce cost substantially. A pressure or velocity Hessian targets interpolation error. An adjoint weights local residuals by their effect on a chosen output, such as torque or pitching moment. Different outputs can select different cells. A vorticity indicator can miss a diffused tip vortex once its peak falls below the threshold. For a transient solver, retain a minimum wake resolution and adapt over a time window. Compare achieved error at fixed cell budget and wall time, including field transfer. [NASA, goal-based metric development](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20210024215) · [Time-accurate anisotropic adaptation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999118304212)

**Embedded boundaries and scaling.** Cut cells can represent an immersed surface while retaining a Cartesian bulk mesh. Very small fluid fractions restrict an explicit solver's time step. Berger and Giuliani demonstrate conservative state redistribution for this problem; their later steady-state-preserving variant is a 2026 preprint with two-dimensional examples. Both require solver participation. A native cut-cell backend would need volume fractions, apertures, cut-face geometry and an agreed stabilization scheme. Separately, p4est demonstrates partitioning, refinement and 2:1 balancing with a forest of octrees. Its scalability results motivate sparse leaf storage. The current native classifier stores full finest-grid arrays, so one extra level multiplies their size by eight at fixed domain. [Berger and Giuliani, cut-cell redistribution](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2020.109820) · [Steady-state variant, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2610.06459) · [Burstedde et al., p4est](https://doi.org/10.1137/100791634)

**Implication for the Rumoca propeller solver.** The uniform lattice solver streams populations to neighbours on a fixed stencil. A general tetrahedral or fitted polyhedral mesh can be consumed by a finite-volume discretization; switching the existing lattice solver to locally refined Cartesian blocks also requires fine/coarse population transfer and a compatible time schedule. Lagrava et al. show why filtering and reconstruction at the transition matter to accuracy and stability. Compare solvers using the same physical near-blade spacing, wall treatment and wake stations, and report mesh convergence for each discretization. [Lagrava et al., multi-domain lattice refinement](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2012.03.015)

**Curved and moving elements.** A curved element can invert inside its reference domain even when sampled corners are well positioned. Johnen et al. bound the determinant of the mapping using a Bézier representation. That is an appropriate validation target for future high-order geometry. Moving linear meshes require separate checks. For each cell, the sum of outward swept-face volumes should equal its volume change. The new native function integrates the area swept by planar triangle fans exactly for linearly moving vertices. A time integrator must use a consistent mesh flux at its integration stages. Load and displacement transfer across nonmatching structural and fluid meshes also need a work balance. [Johnen et al., geometric validity](https://orbi.uliege.be/handle/2268/113717) · [Farhat et al., discrete geometric conservation](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999101969323) · [Conservative interface load transfer](https://www.sciencedirect.com/science/article/abs/pii/S0021999106000891)

## What to validate for each application

| Case | Mesh experiment | Experimental comparison and acceptance evidence |
| --- | --- | --- |
| ONRT marine propeller | Match blade geometry, advance ratio, RPM, wall treatment, rotor reference frame, domain and time step. Vary surface resolution, wall-normal resolution and downstream resolution independently. | Compare thrust and torque coefficients against the IIHR open-water curves. At several axial stations compare axial and tangential velocity, wake width under a common definition, and integrated flux or momentum. A shrinking constant-speed surface alone cannot distinguish contraction, mixing and numerical diffusion. Label any wake quantity lacking measurements as a solver-to-solver comparison. [IIHR open-water geometry and curves](https://simman2020.kr/download/propeller_IIHR2%28ONRT%29.zip) |
| Drone hover and forward flight | First isolate one rotor. Then resolve all four rotors, their relative rotation sense, the space between the disks, and the merging or skewed wake. Repeat at finer azimuthal steps and downstream spacing. For ground-effect cases, also refine the ground jet. | NASA HVAB provides blade airloads and PIV for an instrumented single rotor. The free-flying quadcopter experiment measures a volumetric velocity field and wake merger. Compare thrust, tip-vortex trajectory and core size, velocity profiles and merger distance at matched operating conditions. A 2026 preprint also reports synchronized vortex merging, suggesting a demanding time-resolved follow-up. [NASA HVAB](https://rotorcraft.arc.nasa.gov/HVAB/) · [Free-flying quadcopter measurements](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00348-024-03880-3) · [Synchronized merging, preprint](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.19507) |
| Sailboat aerodynamic wake | Preserve luff, leech, head and jib–main slot. Use the measured flying shape and apparent wind. Exercise attached upwind flow and separated downwind flow separately. A zero-thickness sail needs a two-sided surface representation with independent pressure and suction faces. The closed-STL workflow currently needs a resolved finite-thickness body. | Compare chordwise pressure on each side at multiple heights and integrated drive/side force. For a wake claim, also compare measured vortex locations or velocity sections. Align sampling and normalization, including the distinction between water-channel and air measurements. The cited sail experiments establish benchmark geometries; they do not validate the Hobie 16 geometry automatically. [Viola, Bot and Riotte, wind-tunnel pressure validation](https://www.pure.ed.ac.uk/ws/portalfiles/portal/11894562/Viola_1.pdf) · [Downwind sail vortex measurements](https://www.cambridge.org/core/journals/flow/article/vortex-flow-of-downwind-sails/7D33E98C997AFF463C9AE425D5C5F481) |
| Wing flutter | Start with prescribed pitch and plunge. Refine the leading edge, trailing edge, wall layers and, for transonic cases, the shock-motion envelope. Test uniform-flow preservation on a moving mesh, minimum volume/Jacobian throughout a cycle, and work transferred across the interface. Then couple the measured structural modes. | NASA's BSCW archive includes both forced oscillations and flutter. Compare unsteady pressure amplitude **and phase** before the coupled damping-versus-dynamic-pressure sweep. Refine space, time step and coupling tolerance independently. Compare the zero-damping crossing and frequency with the tunnel results. The X-56A workshop shows that methods with similar predicted speeds can still miss the measured onset. [NASA BSCW experimental data](https://nescacademy.nasa.gov/workshops/AePW2/public/BSCW/experimentalData) · [NASA AePW-3 flight-test comparison](https://ntrs.nasa.gov/citations/20230016485) |

## Evidence required before advertising production support

1. **Geometry and topology.** Measure surface error, retained feature and gap dimensions, patch identity, intersections, cell closure and positive orientation. Include open sheets, thin blades, slotted sections and deliberately invalid inputs. Store the input geometry and meshing settings with each result.
2. **Mesh quality.** Report distributions and worst cells for skewness, non-orthogonality, determinants and interpolation weights, plus first-layer height and achieved thickness. Conforming transition cells and local snap backtracking let the 123,824-cell unlayered propeller pass the extended OpenFOAM v2412 checks. Its surface area differs from the input by −5.55% at the maximum snap setting. The short-domain two-layer propeller still produces inverted face pyramids and the native exporter rejects it. Local layer termination and blade-section fidelity remain qualification priorities.
3. **Discretization and physics.** Use at least three systematically related resolutions when estimating observed convergence. Keep solver settings, physical models and comparison frames explicit. Check wake profiles and pressure phase as well as integrated loads. A better-looking vortex or a similar cell count cannot supply an uncertainty estimate.
4. **Cost and reproducibility.** Measure input conditioning, mesh generation, peak memory, export, solver time and total time to the requested output accuracy. Preserve failed cases and hardware details. Compare native, imported and reference meshes on the same physical cases. The Modelica compatibility gate currently passes in OpenModelica; Rumoca 0.10.4 reports EF023 during flattening of a record-dependent array dimension.

For the next native implementation, the highest common payoff is feature and gap preservation plus a local quality gate during snapping and layer growth. Sparse storage follows for larger wake domains. Rotary interfaces, two-sided sails and a work-consistent moving-wing coupling each need their own solver integration and experimental qualification. The tested entry points and first runnable example are in the [meshing guide](meshing.md).

### Propeller blade and tip-wake qualification

For the ONRT port propeller the geometry inspection compares the wall exported by the native volume mesher to its closed 12,000-triangle CAD-derived input. Capturing every intersected boundary cell gave one connected shell but increased area by 36.3% and enclosed volume by 21.9%. Restricting additional cells to places where the two neighbours along the dominant triangle-normal direction miss the solid reduced these errors to 20.5% and 5.0%. Both evaluations use the same input and grid spacing. Wall area and volume are geometry diagnostics. They give no direct bound on thrust or torque error.

A blade-fitted block or a curvature-sized surface mesh with wall-normal layers is the next comparison for blade loads. Inspect pressure- and suction-side sections at fixed radii, leading-edge radius, trailing-edge thickness and tip shape. Check achieved wall spacing against the turbulence model. Viitanen et al. used blade-fitted O-O blocks and concentrated resolution in the tip-vortex region. [Viitanen et al. (2018)](https://doi.org/10.3390/jmse6020056)

Compare mean thrust and torque across advance ratios on at least three systematically refined grids. For transient wake transport, repeat with decreasing time steps and compare downstream velocity profiles. The Iowa workshop provides isolated-propeller characteristics. The Potsdam VP1304 is a separate geometry with published laser Doppler velocimetry and open-water loads. [W2025 ONRT](https://w2025.nl/instructions/onr-tumblehome-onrt/) · [SVA Potsdam benchmark](https://www.sva-potsdam.de/en/pptc-smp11-workshop/) · [ITTC verification and validation procedure](https://www.ittc.info/media/11950/75-03-01-01.pdf)
